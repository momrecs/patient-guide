Before we begin

🧭 Ground rules

Treatment length isn't luck. It comes down to three habits — and one phone number for everything else.

🪢
Do

Wear your elastics as prescribed. Every skipped month of elastics adds a month of treatment.

🪥
Do

Keep your teeth clean. Teeth don't move well through inflamed gums — and white marks are permanent.

🍬
Don't

Eat hard or sticky things. Each broken bracket adds about 6 weeks. Full list in What can I eat?

🤐
Don't

Sit on a problem. Loose bracket? Poking wire? Tell us early — small fixes stay small.

Something feel wrong? Text us a photo at 1-888-844-9093 with your name. We'll reply within 1–2 business days — usually much sooner.

Yes, we will ask if you've been wearing your elastics. Yes, we can tell. The teeth keep receipts.

The owner's manual

🛠️ My appliance

Tap yours. Every guide covers the same three things: what it does, what to expect, and your part in making it work.

The classic

Your part in getting them off on time comes down to three things: wear your elastics as prescribed, keep your teeth clean, and protect your brackets from hard and sticky foods.

1The first week: sore teeth are normal (again for a day or two after adjustments) — it means your teeth are moving. Eat a good meal on bonding day before the soreness sets in, go soft for a few days, and use your usual pain reliever if you need it.
2Cheeks and lips need a week or two to toughen up. Dry any rough spot and cover it with wax; rinse small sores with warm salt water before bed.
3Ongoing: appointments run roughly every 4–12 weeks depending on the stage. Check for loose brackets when you brush — found one? See Something's bothering me.
A broken bracket that goes unreported can add about 6 weeks to your treatment — tell us early.
22 hours a day

They only work while they're in. Out for meals and brushing, in for everything else — including sleep.

1New tray snug and tender for a day or two? Normal — that's the tray working.
2Attachments (small tooth-coloured bumps) help your aligners grip. One pops off? Keep wearing your trays and text us — not an emergency.

Turn as directed

What it does: gently widens the upper jaw by encouraging the two halves of the palate to grow apart at the seam — moving the bones while they're still adaptable, not just tipping the teeth.

1Pressure under the eyes or around the nose when it's turned — normal.
2A gap opening between the front teeth — a good sign! It means the expander is working, and it closes on its own after we stop turning.

Works around the clock

What to expect: some pressure and tenderness at first (soft foods for a few days help), and possibly some cheek rubbing — use the wax or silicone we've given you.

No rubber bands to remember

What it does: a fixed, custom-made bite corrector bonded to your back teeth that guides your lower jaw forward. Sturdy metal, sits far back where it's barely noticeable, and works all the time — nothing to take in and out.

What to expect: a few days of adjustment while chewing and speech get used to it. Your part: brush around it like any fixed appliance, avoid hard and sticky foods, and text us a photo if anything comes loose or breaks.

3–12 months

What they do: small bonded blocks behind your upper front teeth (usually blue, so they're easy to remove later — and no, they don't show when you smile). They open a deep bite, protecting your enamel and lower braces from biting pressure — which actually speeds treatment along.

What to expect: eating feels strange for the first 7–10 days. Softer foods cut into pieces are your friend, and it steadily improves as your back teeth come together.

One ramp came off? With two you can carry on comfortably — mention it at your next visit. If it was your only one, text us to book a replacement.

Your part: brush well around the bands and the pad, skip sticky and chewy candy, and text us if anything feels loose.

6–12 months

Your part: brush around the bands, avoid sticky and chewy candy, and text us if it comes loose.

Your part: if yours is for tongue training, practice keeping your tongue behind the gate — especially when swallowing. Either way: no sticky, hard, or chewy foods, brush after meals, floss daily, and text us if it loosens.

Short-term guests

What they do: tiny elastic "donuts" placed between back teeth to open just enough space to fit bands at your next visit.

What to expect: a something-stuck-between-my-teeth feeling, with achiness starting 4–6 hours after placement and peaking around 24 hours. Taking the pain reliever you'd normally use, per the package directions, about an hour before your appointment makes a real difference.

Your part: brush normally, but don't floss where the separators sit. Avoid gum and sticky candy. If one falls out early, text us which one.

Wear as prescribed

Congratulations — the braces are off! Now the retainer keeps your new smile exactly where it belongs. Teeth shift fastest in the first months after braces, and keep shifting slowly for life — retention is a lifelong habit.

1Removing it: pull down gently and evenly on both sides — clear retainers crack when peeled from one side.
2Out for eating, sports (including swimming), and brushing. When it's out, it's in its case — never a napkin, never a pocket, never within reach of a dog.
3New-retainer quirks (plastic taste, extra saliva, funny speech) fade within 2–3 days. A sore spot lasting more than 3–4 days means it needs an adjustment — text us.
4Don't flip it with your tongue, and keep it away from heat — no hot water, dashboards, or dishwashers.
5Bring it to every appointment — including dental visits, since fillings and crowns change how it fits.
Lost, broken, or not fitting right? Don't wait for your next visit — text us right away. Teeth move fast without it.
The long-haul wire

What it does: a thin wire glued behind your front teeth that quietly holds them straight for the long haul. A tiny bit of settling over time is natural, but with the wire in place and cared for, your teeth stay aligned for as long as it's there.

1Floss under the wire daily with a floss threader or Superfloss, gently up under the gumline. Careful pressure — pushing hard can bend or loosen the wire.
2Don't pick at it, and steer clear of very hard, sticky, or brittle foods that could bend it.
3Comes loose or off? Save the wire, text us, and come in at the first opening — even a brief gap can let teeth shift.
Not sure of your flossing technique? Ask at any visit — we're happy to show you.
Wear it every game

Custom-made from an impression of your teeth, so it fits precisely (and comes in your choice of colours). It only protects your teeth when it's in your mouth — every practice and every game, not just the big ones.

Clean it with a soft brush, mild soap, and warm (never hot) water, and store it in its case.

Retention is a lifelong relationship. Your retainer is very low-maintenance — but it will absolutely notice if you ghost it.

The fridge-door section

🍎 What can I eat?

One rule covers almost everything — and most "forbidden" foods just need a knife first.

The rule: if it's hard, it can break brackets and bend wires. If it's sticky, it can pull appliances loose and feed the bacteria that cause white marks. Everything else is fair game — often just cut into smaller pieces.
Aligner patients: you're the lucky ones — trays come out for meals, so you can eat normally. Just brush before they go back in.

Beef jerky
Bubble gum
Caramel, toffee & gummies
Corn on the cob — cut it off
Hard chips & tacos
Hard pretzels & bagels
Ice — never chew it
Nuts — any kind
Pizza crust
Popcorn
Raw carrots — cut small
Ribs & bone-in meat
Suckers & hard candy
Pens & pencils

Pens and pencils: the classic exam-time snack. Breaks everything. Chew on your study notes instead — metaphorically.

Sugar-free gum is fine — and go easy on sugary snacks and drinks in general. Sugar trapped around braces is how white marks and cavities start.

The soft-food week

For the first week — or after any adjustment — think soft. Most patients are back to (braces-friendly) normal within a week.

🍳

Breakfast

Oatmeal or cream of wheat, scrambled eggs, yogurt, applesauce, soft fruit, smoothies.

🍝

Lunch & dinner

Pasta, mac and cheese, soups, mashed potatoes, rice and beans, crustless bread, eggs, tofu, fish, lasagna, chili, finely cut chicken or ground beef, soft-cooked veggies.

🍨

Dessert

Ice cream, pudding, Jell-O, anything blended.

Yes, ice cream counts as medicine this week.
The fastest way out of braces

🪢 Elastics (rubber bands)

Braces straighten teeth; elastics fix your bite. Worn well, they're your fastest ticket out of braces.

1

Wear them 24 hours a day unless we've told you otherwise — teeth only move with constant, steady pressure. Out just for meals and brushing; loop them on a finger so they go straight back in.

2

Change them 3–4 times a day. Elastics tire out quickly and stop pulling long before they break.

3

Carry spares everywhere. They will snap at the worst moment. Running low? Stop by the office — or grab more from the elastics box outside our front door when we're closed.

4

Never double up to make up for missed hours. Heavy force doesn't move teeth faster — it can actually "shock" a tooth and stall it.

5

Right teeth, right pattern. Your pattern may change as treatment progresses, so always follow your newest card. Not sure? Text us — we'd much rather re-explain than have teeth move the wrong way.

The honest math: every month of skipped elastics is a month added to your treatment. The effort you make equals the result you get.

Sore teeth for the first few days is normal — and it settles fastest with consistent wear. On-again, off-again wear keeps teeth sore and barely moves them. Hooking them on feels fiddly at first; by day three you'll do it without a mirror.

Your personal pattern — which teeth, which strength, when to wear them — is on the elastics card we gave you (and can text you). Photos of the common patterns are coming to this page soon; your card is always the final word.
Five minutes a day

🪥 Brushing & flossing with braces

Braces give plaque new places to hide. Five extra minutes a day is the entire difference between a beautiful result and permanent white marks.

🪥
Brush after every meal and snack — minimum three times a day, two minutes each. Angle the bristles at 45° to clean above and below every bracket. Plaque's favourite hiding spot is between the bracket and the gums — give it extra attention. We like the Philips Sonicare with a small or orthodontic head, but a soft manual brush works well too.
🧵
Floss with a helper: a floss threader or Superfloss (any drugstore) guides floss under the wire — then hug each tooth in a "C" and clean gently below the gumline. Snagged on a bracket? Don't tug; work it free gently.
🌲
The skinny brush (Proxabrush) from your kit handles the tight spots under wires and around appliances. Keep one in your school bag. A Waterpik isn't required, but it's a great bonus — especially for flushing food from under expanders.
Your insurance policy: a fluoride mouthwash every night before bed, after brushing and flossing — then nothing to eat or drink for 15 minutes. It's your best protection against permanent white marks.

Fixed appliances too: brush carefully around all bands, wires, and metal parts. For anything on the roof of the mouth, run the Proxabrush between the appliance and the palate, then finish with a vigorous swish — details in Fixed appliances.

One routine for all of them

🫧 Cleaning retainers & aligners

Hawleys, clear retainers, aligners, Twin Blocks, mouthguards — same care, three speeds.

1

Every time it comes out: rinse it under cool water.

2

Once a day: brush it gently with a soft toothbrush, cool water, and a drop of mild dish soap. Skip the toothpaste — it's abrasive, and over time it scratches and clouds the plastic (those tiny scratches are where germs like to live).

3

Once or twice a week: a deep clean, with either of the two picks below.

The glued-in crowd

🔧 Cleaning fixed appliances

Expanders, Nance, space maintainers, cribs, Forsus, bite correctors, bonded wires.

1Brush carefully around all the bands, wires, and metal parts every time you brush your teeth.
2Roof-of-mouth appliances: run the Proxabrush between the appliance and the palate, then finish with a vigorous swish of water or fluoride rinse. A Waterpik makes this easy.
3Bonded retainer wire: floss under it daily with a threader or Superfloss — gently.
The fix-it school

🆘 Something's bothering me

Tap your problem. Almost everything on this list has a calm answer — and most of it you can handle at home tonight.

Fixable at home

Wires shift as your teeth move — annoying, but almost never urgent.

1Dry the area, roll a piece of ortho wax into a ball, and press it over the poking end. Sugar-free gum works in a pinch.
2If a flexible wire slid to one side, nudge it back to centre, or tuck the poking end away with a clean pencil eraser.
3Very last resort: a clean nail clipper can snip a poking wire end — but text us first if you can.
Still poking, or causing a sore? Text us a photo and we'll book you in.
Tell us soon

Don't panic — this happens, and it's not an emergency.

1The bracket usually stays on the wire. Leave it, or cover it with wax if it bothers you.
2If it comes off completely, save it and bring it to your next visit.
3Text us so we can decide: fix it right away, or fold the repair into your next appointment.
Please don't quietly wait for your next visit — an unreported broken bracket can add about 6 weeks to treatment.
Tell us soon

Expanders, Nance, space maintainers, cribs, bite correctors, Casper — if any of them feels loose, it isn't doing its job and can irritate your cheek or gums.

1Text us a photo. We want to see you soon, but it can wait for regular office hours.
2Skip sticky and chewy foods in the meantime — they're usually what loosened it in the first place.
Forsus came apart? It looks more dramatic than it is. Text us a photo and avoid opening extra-wide until it's sorted.
Normal — it settles

Soreness after new braces, an adjustment, new elastics, a new aligner, or separators is completely normal. It means your teeth are moving, and it settles in a few days.

1Stick to softer foods for a day or two.
2If you need it, take the pain reliever you'd normally use for a headache, following the package directions.
3A warm salt-water rinse soothes tender gums.
Getting worse after 4–5 days, or one very painful spot? That's worth a text.
Fixable at home

Your cheeks toughen up within a couple of weeks of anything new in your mouth.

1Dry the offending spot and cover it with wax. Reapply as needed — it's safe if you swallow a bit.
2Rinse with warm salt water for about 20 seconds before bed to help small sores heal.
A sore that's getting bigger or not improving? Text us a photo.

2Hook broke, or can't get them on? Text us. Not an emergency, but we want to fix it fast so treatment doesn't stall.
Stop and text

Usually harmless
