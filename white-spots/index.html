The honest explanation

🦷 What are white spots?

White spots are areas of early tooth decay — places where plaque sat against the enamel too long and minerals were lost. They're the first stage of a cavity.

Braces create ledges and tight spots that trap plaque. When brushing and flossing don't keep up — even if you thought you were doing a good job — bacteria in that plaque produce acid, and the acid dissolves calcium and phosphate out of the enamel surface. The chalky white patches are what's left: weakened, porous enamel that reflects light differently from the healthy tooth around it.

This didn't happen because of the braces themselves. It happened because plaque wasn't removed well enough around the brackets, day after day. That's not a judgment — it's the cause, and understanding it matters because the same habits that created the spots will make them worse if they continue.

These are not just cosmetic. White spots are the earliest visible sign of a cavity. If the mineral loss continues — from poor brushing, sugary drinks, or skipping this protocol — they can break down into actual holes in the tooth that need fillings. The good news is that at this stage, they're still reversible.
The window we're in

⏳ Why we're acting now

Early cavities can be reversed — but only while the surface is still intact. Once enamel breaks through, there's no putting it back. That's why we're giving you this today.

The short version

Fluoride and calcium can soak back into weakened enamel and harden it again. The process is called remineralization, and it works — but only if you stay consistent with the routine below and stop the damage from continuing. Saliva helps too. It carries minerals and neutralizes acid naturally. If your braces are off, saliva can now reach those surfaces again, which is a head start.

We're telling you this directly because we want to fix it, not because we want to make you feel bad. The protocol works. But it only works if you actually do it.
Morning and night — no exceptions

🪥 Your three-step routine

Do all three steps twice a day, every day. This is the gold-standard fluoride protocol for reversing early enamel damage.

1

Brush with Clinpro 5000 — a high-fluoride toothpaste we carry in the office (also available behind the pharmacy counter at Shoppers Drug Mart). Brush for two full minutes, angling the bristles at 45° into the gumline and around every bracket or surface. Spit well, but don't rinse with water. Then dab a little extra directly onto the white spots with a clean finger.

2

Rinse with X-Pur Optirinse 0.05% fluoride mouthwash for 60 seconds, then spit. Don't rinse with water after. The fluoride film stays on your teeth and keeps working. We carry it in the office, or you can pick it up at Walmart or London Drugs.

3

Apply MI Paste Plus to the white spots. Wait a few minutes after step 2, then use a pea-sized amount on a clean finger or soft-bristle toothbrush. Leave it on for 3–5 minutes, then spit — don't rinse. At bedtime, you can leave it on overnight. MI Paste Plus delivers calcium and phosphate directly into the weakened enamel, on top of what the fluoride is doing.

Each layer builds on the last. The toothpaste cleans and delivers fluoride, the rinse adds another fluoride coat, and the MI Paste tops it off with calcium and phosphate. Spit after each step — but never rinse with water. Nothing to eat or drink for at least 30 minutes after. When you run out, restock X-Pur Optirinse 0.05% at Walmart or London Drugs — or grab another bottle from us.
Milk protein allergy? Skip step 3. MI Paste Plus contains CPP-ACP, which is derived from casein (a milk protein). Steps 1 and 2 alone are still an effective protocol.
If you still have braces: keep up thorough brushing and flossing too — especially between the bracket and the gumline, where these spots start. Use your interproximal brush under the wire every day. The protocol above is in addition to your regular cleaning, not a replacement for it.
Be patient with the process

📅 What to expect

White spots don't reverse overnight. But with consistent care, most patients see real improvement. Here's a rough timeline.

Day 1 1 month 3 months 6+ months

First month

Spots may look more obvious at first, especially right after debond — that's normal. Start the routine immediately. The sooner fluoride gets in, the sooner repair begins.

1–3 months

Spots start to soften and blur as minerals fill back in. The edges become less sharp and the white becomes less stark against the surrounding enamel.

3–6+ months

Many spots fade significantly or blend in. Deeper ones may still be faintly visible but much less noticeable. This is when we reassess next steps.

Every case is different. Shallow spots can resolve in weeks. Deeper ones take longer and may not disappear entirely — but they almost always improve. We'll check your progress at your visits.
If home care isn't enough

🏥 What your dentist can do

If spots haven't faded enough after 6 months of consistent home care, your dentist has cosmetic options. Get the routine going first — these work better on enamel that's already been remineralized.

Icon resin infiltration Minimally invasive

A tooth-coloured resin is drawn into the porous enamel, filling the tiny gaps that make the spot look white. No drilling, no numbing. The spot blends into the surrounding tooth. One appointment, about 20 minutes per tooth.

Composite bonding For deeper spots

A thin layer of tooth-coloured bonding material is placed over the spot to mask it. Works well for spots that are too deep for infiltration alone. Your dentist shapes and polishes it to match the natural tooth.

Ask your family dentist about these at your next cleaning if the spots are still bothering you after a few months of the home routine. They'll tell you which approach suits your situation.

🚨 Our 3-strike hygiene policy

We don't want to take your braces off early. But we will if your teeth are at risk. Here's how it works.

White spots mean plaque is sitting on your teeth long enough to start dissolving enamel. If we keep seeing that at your appointments, we have to act — because leaving braces on teeth that aren't being cleaned properly causes more damage than the braces can fix.

1

Strike 1 — Hygiene warning. We flag the problem, review brushing technique, and give you this protocol. You get the products and the plan. This is your heads-up.

2

Strike 2 — Second warning. If hygiene hasn't improved at your next visit, we document it again and have a serious conversation. This is your last chance to turn it around.

3

Strike 3 — Wires come out. We remove the archwires and pause active treatment. Brackets stay on, but nothing is moving. Treatment resumes only when we see consistent improvement in your hygiene.

If hygiene still doesn't improve after wires are removed, braces may come off entirely. This means treatment ends early — and the teeth may not be in their final position. We don't want that outcome, and neither do you. But we won't keep braces on teeth that are developing cavities.
We'd rather finish your treatment with a great result. Help us help you — brush like your smile depends on it, because it does.
The habits that matter

✅ Do's and don'ts

The protocol does the heavy lifting, but these daily habits control whether the spots improve or get worse.

💧
Do

Drink water throughout the day. It rinses away acids and keeps saliva flowing — your enamel's natural repair system.

🧀
Do

Eat calcium-rich foods — cheese, yogurt, milk. They supply the raw minerals your enamel needs to rebuild.

⏰
Do

Be consistent. Twice a day, every day. Skipping days gives the acid bacteria a head start again.

🥤
Don't

Sip sugary or acidic drinks — pop, juice, sports drinks, energy drinks. They bathe your teeth in acid and undo the remineralization you're working for.

🍬
Don't

Snack constantly. Every time you eat, your mouth turns acidic for about 20 minutes. Fewer snacking sessions means more time for repair.

🚿
Don't

Rinse after fluoride. This is the most common mistake. Spit — don't rinse — or the fluoride washes straight off before it can absorb.
